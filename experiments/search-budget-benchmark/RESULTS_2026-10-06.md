# Search Budget Falsification — 2026-10-06

## Question

The first 12-domain run suggested replacing one 1,000-generation lineage with many shorter bloodlines. This follow-up tried to falsify that conclusion under a fixed evaluation budget.

## Comparison

Population was fixed at 64 with 12 survivors. Each strategy spent the same 1,000 generation-equivalents:

- 1000 × 1
- 500 × 2
- 200 × 5
- 100 × 10
- 50 × 20
- hybrid: 500 × 1 plus 50 × 10

Two deliberately different synthetic search surfaces were used.

### Rugged / deceptive surface

20 deterministic landscapes with local optima and pairwise interactions.

Observed mean best score:

- 1000 × 1: 50.649
- 500 × 2: 52.770
- 200 × 5: 57.411
- 100 × 10: 59.143
- 50 × 20: 60.534
- hybrid: 60.789

The short-restart strategy was much stronger than one long lineage. The hybrid matched or exceeded the best prior strategy on 15/20 landscapes.

### Smooth / cumulative surface

10 deterministic 128-bit landscapes with a smooth gradient and a prefix reward that benefits sequential accumulation.

Observed mean best score:

- 1000 × 1: 192.0
- 500 × 2: 192.0
- 200 × 5: 192.0
- 100 × 10: 192.0
- 50 × 20: 148.05
- hybrid: 192.0

The 50-generation-only strategy failed badly. The hybrid retained full performance.

## Revision

The earlier recommendation "100–200 generations per bloodline" was too broad.

The safer default is now a **barbell search budget**:

1. reserve roughly half the budget for at least one long lineage,
2. spend the rest on many short independent bloodlines,
3. compare cross-seed winners,
4. only increase the long-lineage share when improvement is still occurring late,
5. only increase restart count when multiple lineages plateau at different local optima.

This conclusion is still synthetic. It is a scheduler stress-test, not evidence that the same allocation is optimal for business ideas or scientific hypotheses.

## Next falsification target

Run the same barbell policy on real KODOKU idea genomes with multiple independent evaluators and measure:

- winner stability across evaluator variants,
- winner stability across seeds,
- lineage diversity,
- late-generation improvement rate,
- real-world evidence agreement.

If the same candidate wins only under one evaluator, it should not be promoted downstream.
